   <h2>April 6, 2019 &mdash; Outstanding Duck</h2>

  <div class="poem-text">

<p>
There is a small, gated, mostly Canadian expat community called Eco Lagarto near Venado. Many of its residents are snowbirds who spend their winters enjoying Costa Rica's warm weather. One of them, a woman from Ontario named Tenny, had been volunteering and leading art projects for the local kids at the Clara Vista community center. Before returning home for springtime in Canada, she wanted to have a barbecue and pool party for the kids who had been participating in her art program. Somehow, Julie and I got ourselves invited.
</p>
<p>
Over our first week or so, we had been eating a lot of beans and rice (let’s call it our immersion diet), so it was nice to have a familiar hamburger from the grill, and we met some of the other Canadians from Eco Lagarto. There were 15 kids from Venado, ranging in age from 3 to 15, all excited for the chance to play and cool down in the pool. Our neighbors, Miguel and Argerie, were the only parents from Venado. This left Claire generally the person in charge of the rest of the kids. On the porch, young Aurora curled up in Miguel’s lap and, eavesdropping on his Spanish, I heard him call her his muñeca, his little doll. (The word muñeca amuses me, because its other meaning – wrist – is so unrelated.)  
</p>

<p>
After our barbecue lunch, we shepherded the kids to the pool for a welcome cool-down on the hot, muggy day. Julie and I helped out and played some rousing games of duck-duck-goose with the younger kids. (Note, I dominated and was named outstanding duck for the afternoon.)
</p>
<p>
One thing that stood out to me about the day was the overall behavior of this group of kids. In the house, they had huddled around Claire, and several of the girls helped Tenny clean up after lunch. In the pool, they splashed and played in their little peer groups, and to my eye, they were conspicuously kind and inclusive in their game playing. (I don't always see groups of kids that age getting along quite so well.)  They really didn’t need too much oversight, other than to coax them out of the pool to return home. It was refreshing and encouraging to witness.
</p>
