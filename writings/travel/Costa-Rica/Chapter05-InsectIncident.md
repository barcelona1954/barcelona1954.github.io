   <h2>April 8, 2019 &mdash; The Great Costa Rica Insect Incident</h2>

  <div class="poem-text">

  <p>
Casa Tica, the modest guesthouse we are renting, is a great little setup for our needs. It’s a one-room house, with all the basics, including both air conditioning and hot water in the shower, both luxury features compared to many houses in Venado. However, one thing we definitely share with the common Costa Rican is that we have bugs. And lots of them.
</p>
<p>
The day we arrived and unpacked, we realized we’d have a little bug problem. There were fairly large creeping and flying ants in the cupboard, on the bathroom floor, and generally roaming around the house. However, the situation got a little more icky when Julie flushed the toilet only to see ants wash down the bowl from the inner rim. (I knew something was amiss by the yelp from Julie watching hundreds of ants flow into the Costa Rican sewage system.)
</p>
<p>
The next day, we let the casa caretaker know about the issue, but we took the liberty to buy some insecticide and try to bring the problem under control as soon as possible. Besides spot-spraying bug killer inside the house, I reckoned that if I sprayed the doors, window frames, and the baseboards from the porch-side, I would deter ants from entering. After dinner, I took my spray can to the porch and went to work….the baseboards, a window frame, one door, and then the next.
</p>
<p>
I walked back into the house. Ooooops. Oh. My. God. Dios mío. WTF! 
</p>
<p>
Termites, centipedes, soldier ants (I swear they were packing heavy artillery), and all sorts of bugs were scampering into the house, although scampering suggests they were entering with a little spring in their step. In reality, if bugs could gasp for air, that’s what these guys were doing. Wobbling, weaving, some running over others in a massive stampede of insects into the house. Too many to count. An infestation. A plague. A lot of bugs.
</p>
<p>
Adrenaline rushing through my body, I grabbed the can of insecticide, and I sprayed everything in sight. I sprayed like a madman. A maniac. An insect-icidal maniac. And I left piles of writhing insects, some still wriggling in pools of insecticide.
</p>
<p>
After the killing was done, the next problem presented itself. Being new to the house, we could not find a broom or mop for the floor.  What to do? As cringe-worthy as it was, we resorted to getting on our hands and knees and used big handfuls of paper towels to herd the dead, the dying (and some literally biting to stay alive) into moveable mounds. We pushed them back through the front door and scooched them off the porch. Eventually, the room was mostly clear. 
</p>
<p>
It dawned on me far too late that a throng of insects was apparently living inside the walls and baseboards and that my master plan to coat the perimeter with bug spray (to keep them out of the house!) left them only one route of escape. Into the inner sanctum of Casa Tica. I’d just thrown a canister of tear gas into their abode. 
</p>
<p>
We sat down. Sighed. Opened a beer. Maybe two. Julie took a moment to gloat about how she had brought her slippers to wear, but I had not. (I am normally the planner in the family, but Julie’s the one who anticipated the need for slippers so as not to be barefooted and crunching critters in the middle of the night.)
</p>

  </div>
