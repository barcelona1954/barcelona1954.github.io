  <h2>April 14, 2019 &mdash; Pura Vida</h2>

  <div class="poem-text">

 <p>
The term Pura Vida (literally, pure life) is a common greeting and national slogan of Costa Rica. I don’t know if I use it exactly right, but my impression is that it works anytime you want to indicate that “life is good.” Something like:  
<p>

“That was a rad wave dude. Pura Vida!”
“Those rice and frijoles are delicious. Pura Vida.”
“Have they really increased the recommended serving size of Pepperidge Farm Mint Milano cookies!?! Pura Vida!”
<p>
(Thinking more about it, I’m not sure about the rice and frijoles. While it is the most common food here, it gets old for breakfast, lunch, and dinner. Maybe not so Pura Vida.)
</p>
<p>
So, Julie and I had a genuine Pura Vida moment. We were out investigating the area in our rental car. I wanted to stop at a few places to inquire about the possibility of renting an ATV for the rough roads. Driving through the pueblo of Marbella, we followed a sign for Costa Tour Adventures (http://costatouradventure.com/), where we pulled off the road in front of a well-kept house set back behind a little office kiosk and an iron gate.  The gate was locked and the kiosk empty, but we could hear voices. We walked around the corner and saw some people on a patio in the back of the house. They saw us and said they would be out to meet with us.
</p>
<p>
We were greeted at the front door by Luis, the owner of the family business. After exchanging a few pleasantries in Spanish in which we explained what we were looking for and what we were doing in Costa Rica, he asked us if we’d like to join him on the patio for ceviche and drinks. ¿Por qué no? (Which, in this context, I would roughly translate as, Duh?)
</p>
<p>
We proceeded to enjoy a most pleasant half an hour or so with Luis, his wife, Jessica, and their friends from San Jose, William and Ligia. William had made the ceviche, full of chunks of fish, citrus, and spices. The whole group was so friendly, warm, and welcoming to us, strangers looking for an ATV to rent. I will admit that it gave me pause when William wanted to show us an L. Ron Hubbard web page. However, this was a momentary blip on the radar, and we proceeded to enjoy the company, the conversation, and Luis and Jessica’s lovely patio garden that opened up to lush tropical forest in the back.
</p>
<p>
In the end, Luis gave us contact info for a nearby poultry farmer who owns and rents ATVs, and he shared with us what he thought a fair price would be (compared to normal tourist prices). Julie and I got up to leave, grinning ear-to-ear, but before we could depart, William gave us two enormous mangos and a papaya grown on his farm. And then, Luis and Jessica’s son, a surf instructor for their family business, arrived. He introduced himself and complimented us on our Spanish. We got in the car and stared at one another, amazed at our good fortune to have encountered such lovely people. 
</p>
<p>
And for this experience, we say, Pura Vida!
</p>

  </div>
