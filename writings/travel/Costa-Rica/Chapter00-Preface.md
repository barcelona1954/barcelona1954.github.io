  <h2>Preface</h2>

  <div class="poem-text">

    <p>
    In 2018, after my wife, Julie, retired (I had been retired for a couple years), we started talking about following through on some of our long dreamed-about retirement goals. For many years, we had envisioned retirement as an opportunity to 1) live in a foreign country for a year, 2) live on an island for a year, and 3) live in a major metropolitan area for a year. It seemed we now had the time and means, so we began seriously to check one of these boxes. But what to do, where to go, and how to fill in the details? 
    </p>
    <p>
    We decided that living in a foreign country should be our first target, and started researching places and prioritizing what we wanted out of such an experience. As nascent Spanish learners, we were keen on a Spanish speaking country. And because language learning and cultural immersion were high on our priority list, this naturally led us away from major tourist zones and ex-pat communities. But it was difficult to find alternatives. While discussing this quandary with our well-travelled daughter, Li-Mae, and son-in-law, Michael, they suggested that maybe a volunteer position might work for us. We’d investigated the Peace Corp Response program, which offers travel, immersion opportunities for retired couples, but we didn’t want to travel to destinations at the whim of a Peace Corp placement team. Li-Mae had her own direct experiences traveling and working as a volunteer in Nepal, Peru, and Costa Rica on various projects. Through her college contacts with the University of Colorado Engineers Without Borders program, she’d befriended a young woman, Tammy Stone, who had formed her own NGO, non-profit organization to support development in a small community on the Pacific coast of Costa Rica.  Li-Mae introduced us to Tammy, and in January of 2019, while we were vacationing in Puerto Vallarta, Mexico, we had a conference call with Tammy and her recent on-the-ground coordinator from France, Claire Malfante, to see if there might be an opportunity for us within her organization, Clara Vista. We chatted about our skill sets and backgrounds and she described opportunities for us to apply those skills in the pueblo of Venado, Costa Rica. Tammy, who had long-held family ties to the area, offered us rental use of a guest house on her family's property as a place to stay, and said we could teach English and technology skills in the village in support of Clara Vista’s goals. And that we could start as soon as we could be ready. 
 </p>
 <p>
   Julie and I were at once energized and apprehensive by the opportunity. It seemed the idea we’d chatted about over dinners and drinks was staring us in the face, but with that, the reality of leaving our home, our families, our dog... we were seemingly crossing a hanging platform bridge into a darkened forest where we couldn't see what was lurking. Determined we were, however, to follow through. We found temporary renters for our house , and they would also care for our dog. We paid outstanding bills and tied up loose ends. We booked flights and, after a short stop in Long Beach to visit our son, Drew and his girlfriend (now wife), Marie, we flew from Los Angeles to Atlanta, to Liberia, Costa Rica. Unbeknownst to us, an adventure stretched out in front of us like a Pacific beach horizon.  In these journal entries, originally written in 2019, I’ve tried to capture and highlight the experiences that cemented themselves in our heads and hearts. 
</p>

  </div>
