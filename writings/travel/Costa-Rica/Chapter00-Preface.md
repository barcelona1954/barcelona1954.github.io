  <h2>Preface</h2>

  <div class="poem-text">
<p>
 In 2018, after my wife, Julie, and I had both retired, we started talking about following through on some of our long dreamed-about retirement goals. For many years, we had envisioned retirement as a trio of activities that would by their nature be adventures in travel. First, we wanted to live in a foreign country for a year. Next, we wanted to live on an island for a year. And lastly, we wanted to live in a major metropolitan area for a year. It seemed we now had the time and means, so we began seriously to check one of these boxes. But what to do, where to go, and how to fill in the details? 
</p>
<p> 
We decided that living in a foreign country should be our first target, and started researching places and prioritizing what we wanted out of such an experience. As nascent Spanish learners, we were keen on a Spanish speaking country. And because language learning and cultural immersion were high on our priority list, this naturally led us away from major tourist zones and ex-pat communities. But it was difficult to find alternatives. While discussing this quandary with our well-traveled daughter, Li-Mae, and son-in-law, Michael, they suggested that a volunteer position might work for us. Li-Mae had her own direct experiences traveling and working as a volunteer in Nepal, Peru, and Costa Rica on various projects. She connected us with a college Engineers Without Borders colleague, Tammy Stone, who had since formed her own non-profit organization in Costa Rica.  In January of 2019, while we were vacationing in Puerto Vallarta, Mexico, we had a conference call with Tammy and her recent on-the-ground coordinator from France, Claire Malfante, to see if there might be an opportunity for us within her organization, Clara Vista. We chatted about our skill sets and backgrounds and she described opportunities for us to apply those skills in the pueblo of Venado, Costa Rica. Tammy, who had long-held family ties to the area, offered us rental use of a guest house on her family's property as a place to stay, and said we could teach English and technology skills in the village in support of Clara Vista’s goals. And that we could start as soon as we could be ready. 
</p>
<p>
Suddenly, our frequent dinner-talks about retirement and living in another country became a concrete possibility. Julie and I were at once energized and apprehensive by the opportunity. What would we do with our home, our cars, our dog? The daily puzzle pieces of our lives would be scattered and reassembled in who knows what shape. However, we were determined to follow through. We found temporary renters for our house, and they would also care for our dog. We paid outstanding bills and tied up loose ends. We booked flights and, after a short stop in Long Beach to visit our son, Drew and his girlfriend (now wife), Marie, we flew from California to Costa Rica. We had little idea what the next several months would bring. In these journal entries, originally written in 2019, I’ve tried to capture experiences that cemented themselves in our heads and hearts. And now, with the distance of several years, I’ve revisited them to reflect on – and perhaps make better sense of things– that I didn’t understand at the time. 
 
 
</p>
