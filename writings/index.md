  <summary>My Story</summary>
  <p>
  When I was an undergraduate in college, I took a writing class at UC Berkeley from James (Jim) Gray, the driving force behind the influential Bay Area Writing Project. The class was an eye opener for me and liberating. It focused on expression, creativity, flow, and certain language structures rather than formal grammar and argument. 
  </p>
  <p>
  One day in class, we had a short writing exercise. We were given 10-15 minutes to compose an introductory paragraph in response to a topic written on the chalkboard. 
  </p>
  <p>
  I don't remember the topic, but I do remember being panicky, paralyzed, and embarrassed by the paltry few words and simple, unfinished paragraph I managed to construct. When time was up, we broke into small groups and passed our papers around, reading and rating one another's work until everyone had evaluated every paper. 
  </p>
  <p>
  When we were done, Jim collected the "ratings" and posted them on the chalkboard. Given the sparse number of words I'd managed to scribble down, I dreaded seeing the results. To my utter disbelief and amazement, however, my paper was the hightest rated in the class. (In retrospect, I guess I'd managed to turn a phrase that sang to people. It didn't matter that it was incomplete or lacked girth.)
  </p>
  <p>
  I look back on that single experience as one that helped me believe in myself as a writer and creative spirit. I went on to write an essay or two for the college newspaper (imagine the voice of Sam Spade as political commentator), tutored and taught writing, and gained confidence in my creative self. 
  </p>
  <p>
  Over time, I found joy and clarity in writing about the world around me and the world I've experienced. (Damn hard and time consuming, though!) Telling my stories has a current that I like to  float in. Fiction, on the other hand, has proven to be more elusive, so I've mostly stayed with what I know: experiences, observations, and reflections that occasionally find their way onto the page.
  <p>
  Some of those writings have found a home here, along with an occasional poem from a kindred spirit, Godric Saint.
  </p>

  </p>
</details>
