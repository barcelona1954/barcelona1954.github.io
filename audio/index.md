<details class="pv-reveal">
  <summary>My Story</summary>
<p>
  I remember watching The Beatles premier on The Ed Sullivan Show in 1964. I was at my Grandparents house, and it was a cold and gloomy February evening in southern Indiana. But that music. And those screaming girls. And that "long hair" on the lads from Liverpool that seemed to shock the adults in the room. I guess these things left an impression. Although my Dad was a an excellent singer...a skill he'd developed from his Aunt / music teacher in his school days, my family wasn't otherwise particularly musical, and there wasn't any live music or instruments in our house growing up. But by they time I was in 4th grade or so, the "British Invasion" was in full swing and I decided I wanted to be part of the action. If only I could play Day Tripper or Ticket To Ride, I was sure my life and elementary school prestige would launch.  With that, I convinced my parents to let me take guitar lessons. 
</p>
<p>
  We somehow scored a cheap acoustic guitar and I started learning guitar from an a crew-cut cowboy, more Buck Owens than John Lennon. Regardless, he taught me to read music and play basic chords, and I developed muscle memory for moving my fingers around the fret board. Lamentably, I was mired in a Mel Bay guitar book with songs like Greensleeves and the Marine's Hymn rather than the songs I heard on AM radio. Those were the ones that I wanted to play. So, after a year or so, I quit my guitar lessons. 
  </p>
<p>
Then, in high school, I found myself with several long-haired friends who were all guitar players and musically inclined. I bought a new acoustic guitar and started learning songs...Bob Dylan, Donovan, Leonard Cohen. The muscle memory from my youth served me well, but I  didn't know much about music per se.... I didn't know what it meant to play in key, or any basic theory or musical scales that would have helped me. So, again, after a couple years of fiddling around with the guitar, I kinda dropped it to peruse art and travel. 
</p>
<p>
It was many years later, when my kids, who were all accomplished musicians, encouraged me to play guitar again.  I got a jump-start by coaxing a co-worker, friend, and accomplished musician, Craig, to give some after-work guitar tutorials to folks who were interested. And then I signed up with a guitar teacher at Graner Music where I always dropped off my kids for their various lessons. My family 
then bought me an Ovation acoustic guitar for Xmas, and I began establishing a routine of practice. 
</p>
<p>
One day at work, this was probably in the early 2010s, I was chatting next to the break-room coffee machine with my friend, Brian, and I mentioned that I was taking guitar lessons. What a coincidence! He mentioned that he was taking bass guitar lessons. We decided to try playing some songs together. I had never played music with anybody else before, but I thought this might be fun, so not long afterwards, Brian came over to my house one evening in the middle of winter and we gave it a go. </p>
<p>
Now, because I thought bands were supposed to practice in the garage, I had pulled out one car to make room for our amps and a seat or two, and that's where we played a pathetically poor man's version of Santana's Europa (I don't recommend starting with that one!) and one of the Creedence Clearwater "Rain" songs. It wasn't until our fingers were chilled and limbs shivering that I had the epiphany: hey, I own this house and we can play in any room I want! So for the next several years, we traded off learning and playing songs every two or three weeks at each others's houses - indoors! 
</p>
<p>We never performed or tried to play outside of our living rooms, but for fun, we came to call ourselves Al Palomino and the Pintos. And over time, we built up a playlist of maybe 15 or so songs that we could muddle through over cocktails and Friday night happy hours. Then, one day, Brian had the idea that we should try an experiment.....He asked me to bring my guitar and amp to his house, and he invited a mutual friend, Chuck, who had played in bands since he was knee-high to a harpsichord to join us. The challenge....while our wives went out to dinner and drinks, we would learn a song and then play it for them when they came home. I was skeptical, but game to try. We picked a song called Surf Nouveau (originally by a group called The Aqua Velvets). Thanks much to Chuck's amazing musical ear and Brian's sense of rhythm, we found the chord progressions and the melody lines and found a selection on our drum machine that worked well enough. We played through a dozen times or so, slowly at first, and then at a tempo close to the original version. Then, when the ladies returned from their girls night out, we played it for them. Not bad. And then we played it again. Kinda fun. And, by popular request, we played it again. The ladies were dancing. Okay....I'm starting to feel those Ed Sullivan vibes, and that's how the Corpse Revivers (Brian, Chuck, a drum machine, and me) were born. 
</p>
