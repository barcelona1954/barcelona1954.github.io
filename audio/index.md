<details class="pv-reveal">
  <summary>My Story</summary>

  I remember watching The Beatles primiere on The Ed Sullivan Show in 1964. I was at my Grandparents house, and it was a cold and gloomy February day in sourthern Indiana. But that music. And those screaming girls. And that "long hair" on the lads from Liverpool. I guess these things left an impression. Although my Dad was a an excellent singer...a skill he'd developed from his Aunt / music teacher in his school days, my family wasn't otherwise particularly musical, and there wawn't any live music or instruments in our house growing up. But by 4th grade or so, I decided I wanted to part of the action and asked my parents if I could take guitar lessons. If I could play Day Tripper or Ticket To Ride, I was sure my life and elementary school prestige would launch.  

  With that, we somehow scored a cheap anoustic guitar and started lessons with a cowboy with a military crew-cut guitar instructor. I learned to read music, and developed muscle memory for basic chords. But I was mired playing Greensleeves and the Marine's Hymn rather than the Beatle and othee popular rock songs I was hearing on the radio. So, after a year or so, I quit my guitar lessons. 

Then, in high school, I found myself with several friends who were guitar players and musically inclined. I bought a new anoustic guitar and started learning songs. The muscle memory from my youth served me well, although I really didn't know much about music per se. I didn't know what it meant to play in key, or any basic theory or musical scales that would have helped me. So, again, after a couple years of fiddling around with the guitar, I kinda dropped it to puruse art and travel. 



</details>
